About

I help brands and creators think past the single post — to the system that keeps an audience coming back, and the packaging that gets them to click in the first place.

I'm Ujjawal Kumar Das, a content strategist and social media growth specialist based in India, working worldwide. I started in the edit bay in 2021, and four-plus years in, that hands-on production background is exactly what makes the strategy work — I know what's actually achievable in the timeline, not just what looks good on a slide.

Today that means owning the content pipeline for CapMint's two YouTube channels, ongoing strategy work with The Clips Agency and YaaS.Media, and a community I built myself at atova.club — now 791 editors strong, grown with zero ad spend. My own page, @kumarrujjawal, is where I test every format before it goes to a client: 14.5K followers, entirely organic.

Every content system I build runs through the same lens: strategy first, execution second. Hooks and retention data shape the plan; Premiere Pro, After Effects, Photoshop and Midjourney shape the output; ChatGPT and automation are woven in wherever they earn their place — never a replacement for instinct built from doing this daily.

FAQ

Questions people ask before we work together.

Everything I usually get asked before a project kicks off.

I work across both strategy and execution, but my strength is connecting the two.

I can help with content strategy, content pillars, ideation, formats, audience positioning, YouTube strategy, and content planning — and I also understand the execution side deeply because I've personally handled scripting, editing, motion design, production, publishing, and performance analysis.

So I don't just create a strategy and hand it off. I understand how to turn that strategy into content that actually gets produced and published.

Yes. I've worked on content systems where I've been involved from ideation and planning to production, editing, publishing, and performance analysis.

I've also managed content teams — including coordinating editors, reviewing scripts, analyzing YouTube performance, planning content, and managing the end-to-end production pipeline.

My approach is to build a system where content isn't dependent on one person constantly figuring out what to post next.

My strongest platform is YouTube, especially long-form and short-form video.

I also work with Instagram/Reels and other short-form platforms, and I understand how to adapt the same core idea into different formats rather than simply reposting the exact same content everywhere.

I've personally built an audience on Instagram as well, so I understand the creator side of content in addition to the brand/business side.

Yes — this is actually one of the areas I'm most interested in.

I'd start by understanding the brand, target audience, positioning, business objective, and competitive landscape. From there, I'd build:

  • Content pillars
  • Audience/content buckets
  • Content formats
  • Ideation system
  • Content calendar
  • Production workflow
  • Scripting guidelines
  • Editing/visual guidelines
  • Distribution strategy
  • Performance tracking system

The goal isn't just to create 10 good posts. It's to create a repeatable system that can consistently produce good content.

Yes. I've worked with creators and agencies, and I'm open to working with clients globally.

I've also worked across different content styles and industries, so I'm comfortable adapting to different audiences, brand voices, and creative requirements.

I generally prefer to price based on scope and the value of the work, rather than simply charging per video. For example, there's a big difference between:

  • Execution: Editing 20 short-form videos.
  • Content management: Ideation + scripting + production + editing + publishing + analytics.
  • Strategy: Building the entire content system, positioning, content pillars, formats, and growth strategy.

So I'd first understand the scope, number of platforms, content volume, level of involvement, and business goals, and then propose a package accordingly.

The easiest way is to have a short conversation about what you're trying to achieve with content, where you are currently, and what's not working.

From there, I can identify where I can add the most value — whether that's content strategy, building the system, managing production, or executing the content itself.

If it makes sense, we can then define the scope, deliverables, timeline, and pricing.
